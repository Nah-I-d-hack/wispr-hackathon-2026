**The problem:** Most care in India starts with a conversation the patient can't fully have. Registration forms, health apps and often the doctor work in English or formal Hindi, while the patient thinks in Bengali, Bhojpuri, Marathi or a mix like Hinglish. The details that matter get lost or guessed: when the pain started, what makes it worse, which medicines they already take. In a crowded OPD the doctor gets a few minutes and half the story.

**Who has it, where and how often:** Anyone who thinks in their mother tongue: a migrant worker in a new city, a farmer at a district hospital, a mother describing her child's fever. It is hardest for older people, who speak slowly, forget details and often need a family member to explain for them. It happens at every visit, at the registration desk, in the waiting line, and on the phone when a family is deciding whether to go to the hospital at all.

**The voice-first product:** Bolo (working name) is a symptom-intake assistant you talk to. The patient or a caregiver presses one button and describes the problem in their own words, in any language or mix. Bolo asks short follow-up questions one at a time, the way a good nurse would: since when, how bad, any old illness, medicines already taken. Then it reads back what it understood in the patient's language and lets them correct it. The doctor or health worker gets a clear English summary with the patient's own words alongside. If something sounds dangerous, like chest pain with sweating or sudden confusion, Bolo stops and tells them to call 112 or go to the nearest hospital. It never diagnoses and never suggests medicine. It works in any phone browser, and on a shared phone or tablet at a clinic desk.

**Why voice comes first:** Writing symptoms in a second language is exactly the barrier we want to remove. Many patients can't read a form, and very few can type Bengali or Hindi quickly on a phone, but everyone can say out loud how they feel. Speech also keeps what forms throw away: the patient's own words for their pain, and a daughter adding "she hasn't eaten since Tuesday". With voice, a 70-year-old can use Bolo without help, and a busy health worker can take a history while their hands are busy with everything else.
